### Master Architecture Blueprint: Enterprise-Grade 4D Spatio-Temporal Neural Rendering Microservices \#\#\#\# 1\. Architectural Foundation & System Evolution Strategy \#\#\#\#\# Context & Strategic Importance Transitioning from exploratory, notebook-bound generative prototypes to a feature-complete, enterprise-grade 4D spatio-temporal neural rendering platform requires moving beyond unconstrained latent visual synthesis. In early generative video implementations, models operate without explicit geometric grounding, treating temporal camera movement as a probabilistic estimation based on 2D frame continuity. This architectural pattern inevitably introduces spatial degradation, camera drift, and structural hallucination over extended temporal sequences. To deliver predictable, production-grade visual assets, the platform must adopt a decoupled microservice architecture. This system anchors temporal neural diffusion to deterministic 6-Degree-of-Freedom (6-DoF) physical camera trajectories, precise geodetic coordinate transformations, and strict spatial provenance boundaries. \#\#\#\#\# System Genesis & Prompting Paradigms Early text-to-video paradigms rely heavily on semantic camera prompting, using natural language descriptors such as "slow pan right," "cinematic drone flyover," or "FPV dive." Semantic prompting introduces fundamental ambiguities: generative models interpret velocity, focal length, axial rotation, and trajectory curvature probabilistically. This results in non-deterministic visual outputs, spatial morphing, and severe degradation of real-world scene identity when the camera returns to previously observed viewports. To eliminate spatial amnesia and structural drift, the architecture treats 3D geometry and camera kinematics as first-class programmatic inputs. The platform decouples visual/stylistic prompting from spatial/kinematic trajectory conditioning: 1\. **Kinematic & Trajectory Conditioning:** The generative model is conditioned directly on deterministic 6-DoF mathematical camera poses derived from real-world telemetry or trajectory spline curves. Positional vectors \$(X, Y, Z)\$ and rotational parameters (yaw, pitch, roll) constrain the view synthesis engine. This preserves property identity, locks architectural boundaries, and prevents structural hallucination across long sequence horizons. 2\. **Visual & Atmospheric Prompting:** Textual prompts are restricted to environmental, lighting, material, and atmospheric descriptions (e.g., "bathed in sharp directional morning sunlight, atmospheric scattering, dense volumetric mist"). Text prompts are stripped of all camera directional directives.  \+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------+ \| INPUT CONDITIONING PARADIGM \| \+--------------------------------------------------+--------------------------------+ \| KINEMATIC & TRAJECTORY LAYER \| VISUAL & ATMOSPHERIC LAYER \| \| (Deterministic 6-DoF Pose Data) \| (Textual / Style Prompt) \| \| \- 3D Position: X, Y, Z Coordinates \| \- Material Properties \| \| \- 3D Rotation: Yaw, Pitch, Roll (Quaternions) \| \- Atmospheric Volumetric Fog \| \| \- Camera Parameters: FOV, Focal Length \| \- Directional Sun Lighting \| \+--------------------------------------------------+--------------------------------+ \| v \+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------+ \| SPATIO-TEMPORAL NEURAL RENDER ENGINE \| \| (Generates High-Frequency Details Locked to Physical Geometry) \| \+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------+  \#\#\#\#\# Operational Paradigms: GEOFORGE vs. Pure Generation To establish clear operational trade-offs between factual site preservation and creative synthesis, three distinct architectural paradigms are evaluated: \| Architecture Type \| Data / Fidelity Trade-offs \| Production Acceptability \| \| \------ \| \------ \| \------ \| \| **Generation First** \| **Pipeline:** Image \$\\rightarrow\$ Generated World \$\\rightarrow\$ Export.

Spatio-Temporal Coordinate Engine & Handshake Protocols \#\#\#\#\# Context & Strategic Importance Directly ingesting geographic coordinates (latitude, longitude, altitude) into local 3D graphic rendering engines introduces catastrophic floating-point precision loss and axial misalignments. Because standard 3D rendering engines operate using 32-bit floating-point numbers, local geometry rendered at global planetary scales suffers from severe coordinate jitter, camera snapping, and structural deformation. The coordinate engine (ges\_converter.py) eliminates these artifacts by projecting geodetic trajectories onto a local metric Cartesian tangent plane. \#\#\#\#\# Geodetic (WGS84) to ECEF to Local ENU Mathematics \#\#\#\#\#\# Step 1: Geodetic (WGS84) to Earth-Centered, Earth-Fixed (ECEF) Projection Given geodetic latitude ( \$\\phi\$ ), longitude ( \$\\lambda\$ ), and ellipsoidal height ( \$h\$ ), coordinates are mapped into 3D Cartesian ECEF space relative to Earth's center of mass using standard WGS84 ellipsoidal constants: \* Semi-major axis ( \$a\$ ) \= \$6,378,137.0\\text{ m}\$ \* First eccentricity squared ( \$e^2\$ ) \$\\approx 0.00669437999014\$ The prime vertical radius of curvature \$N(\\phi)\$ is defined as: \$\$N(\\phi) \= \\frac{a}{\\sqrt{1 \- e^2 \\sin^2(\\phi)}}\$\$ The 3D ECEF position vector \$(X\_{\\text{ecef}}, Y\_{\\text{ecef}}, Z\_{\\text{ecef}})\$ is calculated via: \$\$X\_{\\text{ecef}} \= (N(\\phi) \+ h) \\cos(\\phi) \\cos(\\lambda)\$\$ \$\$Y\_{\\text{ecef}} \= (N(\\phi) \+ h) \\cos(\\phi) \\sin(\\lambda)\$\$ \$\$Z\_{\\text{ecef}} \= \\left(N(\\phi)(1 \- e^2) \+ h\\right) \\sin(\\phi)\$\$ \#\#\#\#\#\# Step 2: ECEF to Local Tangent Cartesian East-North-Up (ENU) Conversion To prevent viewport precision loss, coordinates are shifted relative to an anchor origin point \$(\\phi\_0, \\lambda\_0, h\_0)\$ , defining the local \$(0,0,0)\$ Cartesian center. The delta vector \$(\\Delta X, \\Delta Y, \\Delta Z) \= (X\_{\\text{ecef}} \- X\_{\\text{ref}}, Y\_{\\text{ecef}} \- Y\_{\\text{ref}}, Z\_{\\text{ecef}} \- Z\_{\\text{ref}})\$ is projected onto the local ENU tangent plane via the transformation matrix: \$\$\\begin{bmatrix} x\_{\\text{east}} \\\\ y\_{\\text{north}} \\\\ z\_{\\text{up}} \\end{bmatrix} \= \\begin{bmatrix} \-\\sin(\\lambda\_0) & \\cos(\\lambda\_0) & 0 \\\\ \-\\sin(\\phi\_0)\\cos(\\lambda\_0) & \-\\sin(\\phi\_0)\\sin(\\lambda\_0) & \\cos(\\phi\_0) \\\\ \\cos(\\phi\_0)\\cos(\\lambda\_0) & \\cos(\\phi\_0)\\sin(\\lambda\_0) & \\sin(\\phi\_0) \\end{bmatrix} \\begin{bmatrix} \\Delta X \\\\ \\Delta Y \\\\ \\Delta Z \\end{bmatrix}\$\$  GEODETIC SPHERE (WGS84) (Latitude, Longitude, Altitude) \| v ECEF CARTESIAN COORDINATES (X\_ecef, Y\_ecef, Z\_ecef) \| v LOCAL TANGENT PLANE ROTATION MATRIX (Origin Anchor: Lat\_0, Lon\_0, Alt\_0) \| v LOCAL CARTESIAN ENU METRIC FRAME x\_east \--\> Blender X-axis y\_north \--\> Blender Y-axis z\_up \--\> Blender Z-axis  \#\#\#\#\#\# Real Export Schema vs. Naive Assumptions Raw Google Earth Studio 3D tracking exports (cameraFrames) are frequently misinterpreted: 1\. **Position Frame Misalignment:** The position vector inside cameraFrames is **not** raw ENU metric displacement. It represents ECEF axes shifted to the local-origin track point. Treating raw position vectors directly as metric offsets introduces spatial errors of up to 90 meters. The position vector must be multiplied by the origin's ENU basis matrix \$(\\hat{e}, \\hat{n}, \\hat{u})\$ to derive true local meters: \$\$\\hat{e} \= (-\\sin\\lambda\_0, \\cos\\lambda\_0, 0)^T\$\$ \$\$\\hat{n} \= (-\\sin\\phi\_0 \\cos\\lambda\_0, \-\\sin\\phi\_0 \\sin\\lambda\_0, \\cos\\phi\_0)^T\$\$ \$\$\\hat{u} \= (\\cos\\phi\_0 \\cos\\lambda\_0, \\cos\\phi\_0 \\sin\\lambda\_0, \\sin\\phi\_0)^T\$\$ 2\. **Exact Geodetic Telemetry:** The coordinate object (latitude, longitude, altitude) provides high-precision coordinates and is the primary reference for positional transforms. 3\. **Euler Angular Rotations:** Rotation parameters do not directly map to aeronautical heading/tilt/roll. They represent Euler rotation angles in degrees evaluated as \$R \= R\_x(x) \\cdot R\_y(y) \\cdot R\_z(z)\$ , matching After-Effects/OpenCV camera framing conventions: \$\$\\text{forward} \= R \\cdot (0,0,+1)^T, \\quad \\text{up} \= R \\cdot (0,-1,0)^T\$\$ \#\#\#\#\#\# Camera Intrinsic Matrix ( \$K\$ ) Specification For a standard 1080p viewport ( \$W \= 1920, H \= 1080\$ ) configured with Google Earth Studio's required \$40^\\circ\$ vertical field of view ( \$\\text{fovVertical} \= 40^\\circ\$ ), the camera focal length in pixels ( \$f\_x, f\_y\$ ) and principal point coordinates ( \$c\_x, c\_y\$ ) are defined by: \$\$f\_x \= f\_y \= \\frac{H / 2}{\\tan(\\text{fovVertical} / 2)} \= \\frac{540}{\\tan(20^\\circ)} \\approx 1483.638\\text{ px}\$\$ The exact \$3 \\times 3\$ intrinsic camera matrix \$K\$ is formally defined as: \$\$K \= \\begin{bmatrix} f\_x & 0 & c\_x \\\\ 0 & f\_y & c\_y \\\\ 0 & 0 & 1 \\end{bmatrix} \= \\begin{bmatrix} 1483.64 & 0 & 960 \\\\ 0 & 1483.64 & 540 \\\\ 0 & 0 & 1 \\end{bmatrix}\$\$ \#\#\#\#\# Rotational Alignment & The Quaternion Bridge To bypass gimbal lock, intrinsic Euler angles are converted into normalized unit quaternions. Telemetry arrays stream rotational inputs as q\_in \= qx, qy, qz, qw, where index 3 represents the scalar \$q\_w\$ . Blender's native mathutils engine expects quaternions structured explicitly as \$(w, x, y, z)\$ . correction matrix ( \$R\_{\\text{CV} \\rightarrow \\text{GL}}\$ ) is injected to map vectors into OpenGL/Blender space ( \$Z\$ points back, \$Y\$ points up, \$X\$ points right): \$\$R\_{\\text{CV} Overlapping Trajectory Slicing & Spatio-Temporal Cosine Stitching \#\#\#\#\# Context & Strategic Importance Generating continuous, infinitely long drone flyovers in a single generative pass is computationally unfeasible due to hardware VRAM limitations. To process extended trajectories, global flight paths must be divided into smaller, overlapping temporal slices. However, standard linear cross-fading between rendered video slices introduces luminance drops, contrast dipping, and double-image ghosting. The system resolves this using sliding-window trajectory slicing combined with Cosine Variance-Preserving temporal matrix fusion. \#\#\#\#\# Sliding-Window Trajectory Slicing (infinite\_stitcher.py) Given a global flight path of length \$N\$ frames, a sliding window is defined using chunk size \$C\$ and temporal overlap width \$O\$ . The window stride \$S\$ is calculated as: \$\$S \= C \- O\$\$ For any chunk index \$i \\ge 0\$ , global frame boundaries \$G\_{\\text{start}}, G\_{\\text{end}}\$ are defined by: \$\$G\_{\\text{start}} \= i \\cdot S, \\quad G\_{\\text{end}} \= \\min(i \\cdot S \+ C \- 1, N \- 1)\$\$  GLOBAL TRAJECTORY FRAME SEQUENCE (Length N) \[Frame 0 \--------------------------------------------------------------------\> Frame N\] CHUNK 0: \[Global Frames 0 to 23\] (Relativized to 0-index space: \[0 to 23\]) \|======== Overlap O=8 \========\| CHUNK 1: \[Global Frames 16 to 39\] (Relativized to 0-index space: \[0 to 23\]) Stride S \= C \- O \= 16 frames  Internal frame indices within each extracted chunk are relativized back to 0-index space \$0, C \- 1\$ . This allows headless Blender instances and ComfyUI workers to render isolated sequence blocks without global offset anomalies. \#\#\#\#\# Cosine Variance-Preserving (VP) Matrix Fusion Mathematics Standard linear cross-fading ( \$I\_{\\text{blend}} \= w \\cdot I\_A \+ (1-w) \\cdot I\_B\$ ) reduces high-frequency pixel variance in transition zones, producing visible contrast dips and blurry double-image ghosting. For a normalized transition index \$t \\in 0, 1\$ across overlap width \$O\$ , weighting coefficients \$\\alpha(t)\$ and \$\\beta(t)\$ are defined as: \$\$\\alpha(t) \= \\cos\\left(\\frac{\\pi}{2} \\cdot t\\right)^2, \\quad \\beta(t) \= \\sin\\left(\\frac{\\pi}{2} \\cdot t\\right)^2\$\$ Notice that weighting coefficients satisfy constant energy sum constraints: \$\$\\alpha(t) \+ \\beta(t) \= 1.0\$\$ The final blended pixel matrix \$I\_{\\text{blend}}(t)\$ is reconstructed via spatio-temporal matrix fusion: \$\$I\_{\\text{blend}}(t) \= \\alpha(t) \\cdot I\_A(t) \+ \\beta(t) \\cdot I\_B(t)\$\$  COSINE WEIGHTING COEFFICIENT CURVES 1.0 \+---\\ /---+ \| \\alpha(t) \= cos^2(pi/2 \* t) \\beta(t) \= sin^2(pi/2 \* t)/ \| \| \\ / \| 0.5
